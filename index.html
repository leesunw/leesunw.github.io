About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Linguistics at Georgetown University. My research examines bilingual speech, L2 pronunciation, and how accented speech is evaluated across social, linguistic, and technological contexts.

I am especially interested in how beliefs about pronunciation shape human judgments, how these evaluations relate to phonetic properties of speech, and how they compare with AI-based assessment.

Current Research

Dissertation Project
Who Evaluates “Good” Pronunciation?
Examining how accent beliefs, target-language context, and assessment mode shape the evaluation of L2 English and Korean pronunciation across learners, listeners, and AI systems.

Pronunciation Beliefs and Evaluation
Developing and validating measures of learners’ beliefs about accents and pronunciation, and examining how these beliefs relate to self- and listener-based evaluation.

Bilingual Speech and Phonetic Variation
Investigating phonetic variation across languages, dialects, and tasks, including previous work on Korean and English mid-front vowels.
