About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Linguistics at Georgetown University. My research examines bilingual speech, L2 pronunciation, and how accented speech is evaluated across social, linguistic, and technological contexts.

I am especially interested in how beliefs about pronunciation shape human judgments, how these evaluations relate to linguistic properties of speech, and how human evaluation compares with AI-based assessment.

Current Research

Dissertation Project
Who Evaluates “Good” Pronunciation?
Examining how accent beliefs, target-language context, phonological structure, and assessment mode shape the evaluation of L2 English and Korean pronunciation. The project compares self-, listener-, AI-based, and phonetic measures of pronunciation.
Dissertation Committee: Elizabeth Zsiga, Lourdes Ortega, and Okim Kang (Northern Arizona University)

Accent Beliefs and Pronunciation Evaluation
Manuscript in preparation
Developing and validating a survey-based measure of learners’ beliefs about accents and pronunciation, and examining how these beliefs relate to pronunciation perception and evaluation.

Heritage Language Learners and Pronunciation
Mixed-methods manuscript in preparation
A mixed-methods study of Korean heritage language learners integrating survey and interview data to examine learners’ perspectives on pronunciation, accentedness, and language learning.
