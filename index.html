About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Linguistics at Georgetown University. My work focuses on L2 pronunciation and accents, especially how foreign accents are perceived and evaluated by different listeners and assessment modes.

My current dissertation project brings together pronunciation evaluation, phonetic analysis, language attitudes, and AI-based evaluation, focusing on English and Korean in a foreign-language context.

Current Research

Dissertation Project
Who Evaluates “Good” Pronunciation?
Examining how accent attitudes, target-language context, phonological structure, and assessment mode shape the evaluation of L2 English and Korean pronunciation. The project compares self-, listener-, AI-based, and phonetic measures of pronunciation.
Dissertation Committee: Elizabeth Zsiga, Lourdes Ortega, and Okim Kang (Northern Arizona University)

Self-Assessed Pronunciation and Accent Attitudes
Manuscript in preparation
Developing and validating a novel survey, capturing L2 English users' beliefs about accents and pronunciation, and examining how these beliefs relate to pronunciation evaluation.

Heritage Language Accent Ideology
Manuscript in preparation
A mixed-methods study of Korean heritage language learners integrating results from a novel survey and interview data to examine learners’ perspectives on heritage accents and language ideology.
